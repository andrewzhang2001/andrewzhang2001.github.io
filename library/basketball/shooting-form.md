# Shooting Form
{: .no_toc }

Mechanics and cues I'm working on — updated as I learn.
{: .fs-6 .fw-300 }

---

## Drop straight down, not forward

When loading up for a shot, drop down on the same vertical axis as if you were standing upright. I was making the mistake of leaning too far forward on the dip, thinking I'd generate more power by getting lower. But what actually happens is your body has to rotate back upright before you can release, which probably costs power and forces you to compensate with your arms. Keep the dip vertical — power comes from your legs pushing straight up, not from depth.

---

## 90-degree hand position in the pocket

The shooting hand should already be holding the ball at roughly 90 degrees when it's in the pocket (the set point before you go up). If the hand isn't set here, you end up adjusting mid-air before the release, which kills consistency. Getting this right early has two benefits:

- **More consistent follow-through** — your hand is already in its release shape, so there's less to go wrong on the way up
- **More arc** — a properly set hand gives you a platform parallel to the ground at release, which naturally puts more arc on the ball

---

## The lean-back / sway

NBA players lean back when they shoot. I tested this and it immediately felt different in a good way — I was able to get my shooting arm to a position parallel to the ground much more easily.

The explanation I've read is that players jump slightly forward while leaning their upper body back, which lets them generate power from forward momentum while keeping their back and release point stable. But honestly, when I was shooting around today I didn't feel like I was jumping forward much at all. It just felt like the lean itself opened up my form. Need to do more research into whether the forward jump is essential or if the lean alone is doing the work.
